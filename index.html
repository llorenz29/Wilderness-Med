Who we are

Care beyond the trailhead

The Georgetown SOM Wilderness Medicine Group was established with the help of Dr. Matthew Wilson of Georgetown Emergency Medicine. We teach students the basics of wilderness medicine through in-person workshops and online seminars, and we get outside as often as the curriculum allows.

  • Hands-on skills you can practice in a parking lot and use on a ridgeline.
  • Field days on trails and rivers within an hour of the Hilltop.
  • A path forward to the M4 elective, WFA and WFR courses, and the Wilderness Medical Society.

    The Wilderness Medicine elective

    EMED-507 is a two-week M4 elective run by Georgetown Emergency Medicine and offered since 2013. Students spend two weeks learning what to do when help is hours away, finishing with an overnight climbing trip in West Virginia.

    1. Billy Goat Trail, Great FallsEvacuating injured hikers, improvised litters and slings, and "sleeper" scenarios sprung mid-hike.
    2. Potomac RiverSwift-water safety and rescue with professional instructors from Calleva Outdoors.
    3. Classroom daysToxicology, hyperbarics, aerospace and space medicine, disaster triage and sports medicine experts.
    4. Seneca Rocks, West VirginiaOvernight camping, guided rock climbing and campfire capstone talks on toxic plants and animals.

    Course directors

    Maria Lawrynowicz, MD (M'18) and Chloe McCoy, MD, PhD, FAWM, both assistant professors of emergency medicine.

    Topics include

    • Altitude illness, hypothermia and heat illness
    • Drowning and snakebite
    • Hunting and fishing injuries
    • In-flight medical emergencies
    • Disaster triage
